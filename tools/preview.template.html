Every cue in the game, and the one number the whole design hangs on.

The race is a rhythm game wearing a sack-race costume. HOP_TIME 0.30 s, BUFFER 0.10 s and PERFECT 0.18 s pin the combo-sustaining tap cycle to 0.20–0.48 s — so the music is 160 BPM, and the kendang on every quarter note is the tempo that wins. Everything here is synthesised by tools/make_audio.py.

The thesis, audible

160 BPM · beat 375 ms

The pulse plays underneath while a simulation taps at the cadence you pick, running the real hop/land/trip logic from index.html. Listen for whether the taps sit on the drum — and watch the combo. The 120 BPM row is the trap: it is a perfectly natural march tempo that lands outside the combo window.

Music layers

same key, same tempo

One musical idea in four stems rather than five separate tracks, so a state change adds or drops a layer instead of cutting to a new cue. They start phase-locked and stay that way — the drum loop is 3.0 s and the melody 6.0 s, so one divides the other exactly. Hook enters at combo ≥ 4: the music thickens when you are doing it right.

Cues

one sprite sheet · 14 slices

Dashed pads are the moments that are silent in the game today — the trip is the only punishment the race has and it makes no sound at all, so a player who mashes just gets slower with no idea why.

Combo ladder

one file · 12 pitches

COMBO_MAX is 12, which is exactly twelve steps of D major pentatonic. One recorded cue, pitched by playback rate, so the ladder costs no extra bytes — and the rising line teaches the rhythm better than any number on screen.

Synthesised locally — no generative audio model in the loop. Every asset is regenerated by .venv/bin/python tools/make_audio.py && tools/encode_audio.sh, and the whole set encodes to — of AAC. The design, the Higgsfield prompts for the organic layers, and the level table all live in SOUND.md.
